I got a medical bill. Now what?

Most surprise bills happen because the provider never sent the claim to Allied, or sent it to the wrong place. Check the bill against your Allied EOB before you pay anything.

  1. Look at the bill. Does it show a line for "insurance payment," "plan discount," "adjustment," or "network discount"?
    • No such line: the provider hasn't billed Allied. Go to step 2.
    • Yes, and the amount due matches your EOB: pay it.
    • Yes, but the amount due is higher than your EOB says you owe: go to step 4.
  2. Call the provider's billing office Use the number on the bill and say:
    You say"My insurance is Allied Benefit Systems, not Blue Cross or UPMC. Please submit this claim to the address on the back of my card. Can I read you the claims address and my member ID?"
    Read them the claims address, your Group number, and your Subscriber ID from your ID card. Ask them to put the account on hold while the claim processes.
  3. Watch for your EOB. Allied sends an Explanation of Benefits for every claim, by mail or in the My Allied Portal app. It usually arrives within a few weeks of the visit. The EOB is not a bill. It tells you:
    • Billed amount: what the provider charged
    • Allowed amount: the lower price the provider agreed to as a First Health network provider
    • Plan paid: what Kenson's plan covered
    • Your responsibility: your deductible, copay, or coinsurance. This is the only number that should match the provider's bill.
    • Claim number: write it down. Allied needs it for any question.
    • Remark codes, at the bottom: if the claim is denied or pended, the reason is here.
  4. Compare. If the provider's amount due is higher than "your responsibility" on the EOB, you are being balance billed. Don't pay the difference. Get Allied Advocate involved.
  5. If you're stuck, bring the bill and the EOB to Michelle or Emricka. They can check whether the claim ever reached Allied, which tells you in one look whether this is a resubmit problem or an Advocate problem.

Two habits that prevent most of this. Show your Allied ID card at every visit, even at an office you've been to before; billing systems change. And set up the My Allied Portal app so you see claims as they process instead of waiting for mail.

Is my doctor in network?

Your network is First Health, a national PPO. Front desks often say "we don't take that" because they don't recognize the Allied name. Almost every major hospital and practice in western Pennsylvania participates in First Health, so ask the right question.

Before you book, ask the office: "Do you accept the First Health network?"
Not Blue Cross. Not UPMC. Not Aetna. First Health.

If they say no, don't leave. Have them look at the back of your Allied ID card, or call Allied at 800-288-2078 and let Allied confirm it with them.

Check for yourself in under two minutes

  1. Go to alliedbenefit.com/ProviderNetworks and select "First Health Network."
  2. Click "Locate a provider or Create a directory."
  3. Under Network Options, select First Health network and click Start now.
  4. Pick the type of provider: physician, hospital, urgent care, lab and radiology, or all providers.
  5. Search by ZIP code or by state, then click Search now.
  6. Refine the results by specialty or distance if the list is long.

Or open the My Allied Portal app and use Find Care, which also shows cost estimates for common procedures.

Why it matters

In-network providers have agreed to First Health's allowed amounts, so your copay and coinsurance are lower and the provider can't bill you for the difference. Out-of-network providers can bill you the full charge, and that amount does not count toward your out-of-pocket maximum.

Three places people get caught

  • The doctor is in network but the hospital or surgery center isn't, or the other way around. Check both before a procedure.
  • Anesthesiologists, radiologists, and lab work. Ask the office which lab and which anesthesia group they use, and check those too.
  • The office "accepts" First Health but bills to the wrong address. Show your card at check-in every time.

If you can't find a provider you need, call Allied at 800-288-2078 and ask them to find one for you. That is part of what Member Services does.

Getting Allied Advocate involved

Allied Advocate is the team that works a billing problem for you: they contact the provider, straighten out the claim, and negotiate what you owe. You don't have to argue with a billing office yourself. Three triggers, three things to gather, one number.

Call Advocate when

  1. A provider bill is higher than the "your responsibility" amount on your Allied EOB. That's a balance bill.
  2. A provider won't send the claim to Allied, or says they don't accept First Health even after you've shown your card.
  3. You get a collections letter, or a threat of collections, for a plan-covered service.

Also worth a call: a denied claim you don't understand, or a large scheduled procedure where you'd like to know the cost ahead of time.

Gather these three things first

Advocate can't start without them, and the itemized bill is the step that stalls most cases.

  1. The itemized bill with codes. Not the summary statement. Call the provider's billing office and ask for "an itemized statement with procedure codes." It should list each date of service, each service with its CPT or HCPCS code, the charge per line, the diagnosis code, and the provider's tax ID or NPI.
  2. Your Allied EOB for that date of service. Pull it from the My Allied Portal app or alliedbenefit.com, or use the mailed copy. The claim number on it is Advocate's reference for everything.
  3. Any letters from the provider or a collection agency, with dates.

Also helpful: a photo of the front and back of your ID card, the provider's billing phone number and your patient account number, and the best phone number and time to reach you.

One number. Call Allied at 800-288-2078 and ask for Allied Advocate. Monday–Friday 8 am–8 pm Central, Saturday 9 am–noon Central.

What happens next

  • Advocate opens a case and gives you a case number. Write it down.
  • Call the provider once and say: "This bill is under review by my health plan's advocate. The case number is ___. Please hold the account." That usually keeps it out of collections.
  • Pay what you actually owe per the EOB: your copay, deductible, or coinsurance. Don't pay the disputed part, but don't ignore the provider either.
  • Send Advocate any new letter that arrives while the case is open.
  • Ask your advocate how long it usually takes, check the portal, and answer their calls promptly. Most delays are waiting on the member.

Michelle and Emricka can help you gather the itemized bill, find the EOB, and confirm whether Allied ever received the claim.

Brand-name and specialty prescriptions

If you take, or are about to start, a brand-name, high-cost, or specialty medication, SHARx is how you get it. Those medications are not covered when you swipe your insurance card at the pharmacy; SHARx sources them for you instead, usually at a much lower cost to you than the pharmacy price. Generics keep working the normal way at the pharmacy with your ID card.

How you'll know it applies to you. The pharmacy tells you the medication isn't covered, or you get a welcome email or text from SHARx after a fill is attempted. Either one is your signal. Don't pay cash at the counter.

Better: call before your first fill

SHARx works fastest when they hear from you before the pharmacy does. Call 314-451-3555, option 1 if:

  • You're a new hire and already take a brand-name or specialty medication
  • Your doctor just prescribed a new brand-name or specialty medication
  • Your doctor switched you from a generic to a brand-name drug

The four steps

  1. SHARx is notified of the attempted fill, or of your call, and your advocacy case starts.
  2. A SHARx advocate reaches out by text, email, or phone. Watch for it and answer.
  3. Set up your SHARx account at app.sharxplan.com: verify your contact info and complete the HIPAA form so they can work on your behalf.
  4. SHARx finds the best sourcing option for you. Keep in touch through the portal, phone, or email and send requested documents promptly.

Have these ready

SHARx will ask for some or all of them. The sooner they have them, the sooner you have your medication.

  • A photo ID
  • Your household size and household income. Some members are also asked for proof of income, such as a recent pay stub or tax return.
  • Your insurance information: your Allied ID card
  • Your prescribing doctor's name and phone number

While your case is open, don't stop taking your medication. If you're about to run out, tell your SHARx advocate right away and ask about a bridge supply so there's no gap.

Questions at any point: SHARx, 314-451-3555, option 1. Portal: app.sharxplan.com. A three-minute overview video is at sharxplan.com/OEVideo. If you've enrolled, let Michelle or Emricka know; it helps them follow up if anything stalls.

How a normal visit goes

  1. Show your Allied ID card at check-in, every visit. The back tells the office where to send the claim.
  2. Pay any copay the office collects.
  3. Allied processes the claim and sends you an EOB. The EOB is not a bill.
  4. If the provider then bills you, pay only the "your responsibility" amount from the EOB.
  5. A bill that shows no insurance payment, or is higher than your EOB, is a problem you don't pay. See I got a bill and Allied Advocate.

Terms you'll see on your EOB

  • Deductible: what you pay first each year before the plan shares costs. Your amount is on the front of your ID card.
  • Copay: a fixed amount for certain visits.
  • Coinsurance: your percentage of the allowed amount after the deductible.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: the most you pay in a year for covered, in-network care. After that, the plan pays 100 percent.
  • Allowed amount: the discounted price a First Health provider agreed to. In-network providers can't bill you above it.
